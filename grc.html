CASE-01 · Risk assessment & PCI DSS audit GRCVuln Mgmt Team of 6 · Mar 2026

From risk scoring to a PCI DSS audit report

A full audit cycle on two fictional companies: a risk assessment for iBank Financials, then a PCI DSS SAQ-D assessment and audit report for SnowBe Online as it prepares to go public.

  • Risk assessment: listed iBank's assets, threats and vulnerabilities and scored each with Risk = Likelihood × Impact on a 5×5 matrix. Client management servers scored 25 (Extreme) because new hires had full access with no segmentation.
  • Scoping: placed SnowBe at PCI Merchant Level 2 (about 1.2 million card transactions a year) and showed why only SAQ D applies: it stores cardholder data on its own WordPress site.
  • Assessment: walked the SAQ-D requirements and marked gaps: indefinite data retention (3.1), possible stored authentication data (3.2), no change control (6.4), broad access (7), shared location logins (8.1, 8.5), no MFA for remote laptops (8.3).
  • Report: wrote findings with observation, relevance and next steps, plus the AOC and quarterly ASV scan obligations.

My finding
Requirement 6.1, identifying security vulnerabilities: SnowBe needed a permanent process that pulls in external threat sources and ranks vulnerabilities high, medium and low with clear ownership, instead of a one-time consultant cleanup.

CASE-02 · Post-incident gap analysis GRCSOC CIS Controls v8 · Jun 2026

What would have stopped the breach? A CIS Controls gap analysis

A case study breach: an unregistered personal laptop on corporate Wi-Fi carried a worm that reached an anonymous-access FTP server, and a large encrypted exfiltration of proprietary designs was routed to the help desk instead of security.

For each gap I explained how the safeguard would have broken the attack chain. For example, network access control would have dropped the laptop onto a guest VLAN that couldn't reach the FTP server, and a severity matrix would have sent a large outbound transfer straight to the security team.

CASE-03 · Compliance program design GRC 4-week case study · Sep 2025

Mapping iBank Financials to the rules it has to follow

A month-long case study on a growing bank that stores customers' cards, shares office Wi-Fi with another company, lets agents connect personal tablets, and gives new hires full server access.

  • Week 1, obligations: identified the regulations in scope (SOX, PCI DSS, GLBA, NYDFS, SEC, FinCEN AML/KYC) and why each applies.
  • Week 2, controls: mapped iBank to the PCI DSS v4.0 requirements and picked the three most urgent: protect stored account data (3), restrict access by need to know (7), and back security with policy (12).
  • Week 3, privacy: applied the NIST Privacy Framework functions (Identify-P, Govern-P, Control-P, Communicate-P) to iBank's real problems, like blanket access and stored cards.
  • Week 4, domains: walked the seven IT domains from users to remote access and named the biggest risk in each, such as unmanaged BYOD tablets on the VPN.
Regulations
SOX, PCI DSS, GLBA, NYDFS, SEC, FinCEN AML/KYC
Frameworks
PCI DSS v4.0, NIST Privacy Framework, 7 IT domains

Weekly written report and recorded presentation
CASE-04 · Information security plan GRC Team of 4 · v1.0 → v4.0

SnowBe Online security plan

A security plan for a fictional online retailer with a laid-back culture, universal employee access, neglected patches and stored credit cards.

Our team turned a consultant's findings into a plan of 14 policies and 3 standards/procedures, mapped to NIST SP 800-53 access control (AC) families and to PCI DSS because SnowBe stores cardholder data.

  • Replaced "most employees had universal access" with RBAC network segmentation (AC-3) and least privilege (AC-6).
  • Covered remote access for 30 VPN laptops (AC-17), device lock and session termination (AC-11/12), information flow (AC-4) and information sharing (AC-21).
  • Defined roles, an exceptions process with compensating controls, and a version history across four revisions.

My part
Researched and wrote the introduction and definitions sections, and wrote the plan's password standard and procedure (CASE-03).

CASE-05 · Password standard & procedure GRCIT Support Sole author · Oct 2025

Password standard and password procedure

Two companion documents for SnowBe: the standard sets the technical rules, the procedure tells people and admins how to follow them.

The split matters. A standard says what a compliant password is; a procedure says what an employee does on day one and what a help desk technician does when someone is locked out.

  • Assigned responsibilities from CISO (sets parameters) to IT director (deploys enforcement tools) to sysadmins (configure OS, apps and domain controllers).
  • Required admins to verify a user's identity before any reset and to issue temporary credentials that must be changed at first login.
  • Required audit logging when a reset runs, so the ISO can review it.

CASE-06 · Threat modeling across the policy set GRCVuln Mgmt STRIDE · NIST SSDF · CMMC

Shifting security left: threat modeling in SDLC, maturity and policy

A written analysis of where threat modeling fits in SnowBe's System Development Lifecycle, Security Maturity and Patch Management policies.

  • Design: data flow diagrams and STRIDE expose trust boundaries before code is written.
  • Build and test: the model drives targeted controls (input validation against XSS and SQL injection) and points pen testing at the high-risk paths instead of generic scans.
  • Maturity: with BSIMM or OWASP SAMM, threat modeling grows from high-risk projects only to a mandatory design gate, then into CI/CD.
  • Policy: policy says what must be protected; the threat model shows how and where, and feeds the incident playbooks.

CASE-07 · Incident reporting & email analysis SOCGRC OWASP · FERPA · Thunderbird

Writing up a pen-test finding for leadership

An incident report, written as the information security manager, after a third-party penetration test found broken access control and injection flaws in a university's student portal.

  • Documented discovery, validation, scope and duration: an estimated 22,000 students and 80 staff exposed for about six months.
  • Assessed impact on data, systems, money, reputation and regulation (FERPA, GDPR), and classified the information involved by sensitivity.
  • Built a mitigation plan with timelines: WAF virtual patches and IDS within a month, stored procedures, deny-by-default access, server-side authorization checks, and a possible rollback of the faulty update.

Also: email header analysis
In a separate lab I read a message's full headers in Thunderbird and traced each hop between the two mail servers, the virus-scan stamps, the DKIM signature and the message ID, the same skills used to triage phishing.

Findings
Broken access control, injection, privilege escalation
Users
