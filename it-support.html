CASE-01 · Small-office network build IT Support Final project · Dec 2024 · 24 hrs logged

Network implementation for Marconi Law Firm

Scoped, priced, addressed and configured the network for a 10-person law office in Orlando, delivered as a client report and pitch.

  • Specified and priced 28 nodes: 10 laptops, 10 VoIP phones, router, rack server with RAID 5, 48-port PoE switch, 2 Wi-Fi 6E access points, printer, conference phone and smart TV.
  • Designed the addressing plan: a /24 with reserved ranges for computers (.1–30), VoIP (.31–60), network backbone (.80–90), WAPs (.90–100) and shared devices (.100–120), plus a static IP for every node.
  • Configured Windows Server: 10 user accounts, Attorneys / Accounting / Administrative groups, and departmental shared folders with permissions.
  • Troubleshot "user does not have privileges" errors on the shares; the causes were the firewall and overlapping share permissions.
  • Placed every device on the floor plan and kept timesheets and weekly status reports across four milestones.

Scan, patch, rescan: remediating a five-host lab network

Weeks 1–3 of a vulnerability management capstone: inventory the environment, run credentialed and non-credentialed Nessus scans, then work every Critical and High finding down to zero or document why it can't be.

What I did

  • Asset discovery: verified access to the Kali control host, Nessus and the ticketing spreadsheet, created inventory tickets for Nessus, Wazuh and Splunk, checked scanner health, and documented all 20 Nessus scan templates and what each is for.
  • Scanning: ran a non-credentialed and a credentialed Basic Network Scan across all five hosts, plus an all-ports discovery scan. Credentials authenticated on three hosts; I flagged the two failures.
  • Remediation: patched the Server 2019 host, rescanned, then closed the last High (CVE-2013-3900) with the EnableCertPaddingCheck registry fix and rescanned to confirm 0 / 0.
  • End-of-life server: Windows Update on Server 2008 failed on activation. I documented the failure and recommended isolation, a strict host firewall, disabling unneeded roles and SMBv1, and SIEM/EDR monitoring.
  • Logged each remediation in a ticket stating whether OS updates were completed.

When you can't patch: research and controls I wrote up

  1. End-of-life OS Find them from asset inventory and scan output; segment, tighten firewalls, add HIDS/EDR, least privilege.
  2. Legacy libraries Software composition analysis and CVE review; WAF rules, containment, input validation, egress filtering.
  3. Design flaws Threat modeling against MITRE ATT&CK; micro-segmentation, zero trust gateways, MFA, encryption in transit.
  4. Firmware limits Isolated out-of-band management, disable Telnet/HTTP/old SNMP, enforce SSHv2/HTTPS and ACLs.
  5. Embedded / OT CISA ICS advisories; ICS-aware IDS, one-way gateways, hardened industrial firewalls.

This continues in the final report, where the open findings became tracked tickets and I investigated the Kali host.

CASE-04 · Patch and verify before production Vuln MgmtIT Support Nessus · Windows Update · Dec 2025

Hardening the HammerCorp server: 115 findings down to one medium

An OS hardening lab: scan the HammerCorp corporate server (Windows 10), patch it, and rescan to measure what the updates actually fixed.

ScanTotal findingsWhat was left
Before patching115Many Critical, mostly from missing Windows updates
After updates + rescan421 Medium (SMB signing not required, CVSS 5.3) and 41 informational
  • Ran a Nessus scan against the HammerCorp corporate server and concluded it was not safe to put into production: 115 findings, many of them Critical.
  • Applied the outstanding Windows updates and rescanned. Every Critical and High was gone; the only real finding left was a Medium, SMB signing not required.
  • Explained why informational findings still matter: they don't break anything themselves, but they tell an attacker what to look for.

My go-live checklist
Configure the server, firewall rules and Active Directory first, then scan, patch what the scan finds, and run a final scan to catch anything lingering before the system goes into production.

CASE-05 · Password standard & procedure GRCIT Support Sole author · Oct 2025

Password standard and password procedure

Two companion documents for SnowBe: the standard sets the technical rules, the procedure tells people and admins how to follow them.

The split matters. A standard says what a compliant password is; a procedure says what an employee does on day one and what a help desk technician does when someone is locked out.

  • Assigned responsibilities from CISO (sets parameters) to IT director (deploys enforcement tools) to sysadmins (configure OS, apps and domain controllers).
  • Required admins to verify a user's identity before any reset and to issue temporary credentials that must be changed at first login.
  • Required audit logging when a reset runs, so the ISO can review it.
